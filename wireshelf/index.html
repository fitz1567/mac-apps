Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

Privacy PolicySupportAll apps
The short version: Wireshelf does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and none of your work is sent to us. The license and update checks are described below.

This policy explains how Wireshelf (“the app”), a Mac app that keeps a small office network’s records: devices, owners, rooms, address plan, tags and changes, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Wireshelf does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • Your sites’ records (devices, people, rooms, the address plan, the last scan and the change log), in the app’s own folder on your Mac, with daily backups beside them
  • Your settings: the open site, scan pace, reminders and label layout
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

Records are in Wireshelf’s Application Support folder (Settings › Data › Show in Finder), settings in its preferences, and the license key in your Keychain. Imports are read only from files you choose; exports are new files, written only where you choose.

Your files

Wireshelf runs in Apple’s App Sandbox, so it can open and save only where you choose. Your records hold names, addresses and serial numbers; they are read and written on your Mac and never uploaded or sent anywhere. Keep FileVault on.

Scanning

A scan covers only the private network this Mac is connected to (at most 256 addresses unless you confirm up to 1,024), after you confirm you may scan it. It sends pings and name questions, listens for Bonjour names and, if you tick it, tries 12 common service ports once each. It never logs in, never guesses passwords and never changes a device, and what it finds stays on your Mac.

Network

Wireshelf is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only internet requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the app opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the app’s product ID) and checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com. Neither sends anything about your network or records.

Updates and license checks

Wireshelf, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from fitztoppicks.com only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Wireshelf contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Wireshelf is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
