The short version: Cutwright does not collect your data.

This policy explains how Cutwright (“the app”), a Mac app that adds Cut and Paste, New File, Send To, Copy as Path and a ⌘L address bar to Finder, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Cutwright does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • Your settings (which keys are on, Send To favorite folders, the Terminal app, path style)
  • This session’s Undo History (what Cutwright changed, so it can be taken back); it isn’t saved when you quit
  • Your own New File templates, if you add any
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

Settings and templates are in ~/Library/Application Support/Finderplus on your Mac (the folder keeps the app’s working name), and the license key is in your Keychain. Files Cutwright creates, copies, moves or renames stay wherever you put them.

Your Mac

Cutwright runs outside Apple’s App Sandbox because it creates, moves and renames files wherever you point it in Finder and needs macOS’s Accessibility permission to notice ⌘X, ⌘V, ⌘L and Esc. It looks at those keys only while Finder is the front app and you aren’t typing in a text field, and never records what you type. If macOS doesn’t start that key watcher with Accessibility alone, Setup asks you to switch Cutwright on under Input Monitoring too, for the same keys. To turn either off, switch Cutwright off in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility (or › Input Monitoring); the keys then go to Finder as usual. Its Finder extension is sandboxed and only passes your menu choice and the selected items to the app. With your permission (macOS’s Automation permission for Finder, which Setup asks for) it asks Finder for its front window’s folder and the selected items, and for ⌘L tells that window which folder to show; it sends no other commands. To turn that off, switch Finder off under Cutwright in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation. Nothing about your files is uploaded or sent anywhere.

Network

Cutwright is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the app opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the app’s product ID), checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com and the list of our other Mac apps, downloaded from fitztoppicks.com when you open it (at most once a day; the same file for everyone). None of them sends file names, paths or anything else about your files. Mail Recipient and AirDrop in Send To open Apple’s own Mail and AirDrop; nothing is sent until you send it there.

Updates and license checks

Cutwright, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from fitztoppicks.com only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Cutwright contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Cutwright is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
