The short version: Ejectwise does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and nothing about you or your work is sent to us.

This policy explains how Ejectwise (“the app”), a menu bar app that names what’s keeping a drive from ejecting and ejects it, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Ejectwise does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac only:

  • The history of eject attempts (which drive, what held it, when), up to the last 500
  • Your settings: shortcuts, Time Machine choices and the menu bar options
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

The history is a file in Ejectwise’s Application Support folder; settings are in its preferences and the license key is in your Keychain.

What it reads

To find what holds a drive, Ejectwise asks macOS which of your own processes have files open on it, a folder on it, or were run from it, the same information Activity Monitor and lsof show. It reads names and paths, never the contents of your files, and keeps nothing but the history above. It isn’t sandboxed, because the App Sandbox doesn’t allow listing other apps’ open files; it never quits Finder, the Dock or macOS services, and it asks apps to quit politely.

Network

Ejectwise is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check with Gumroad (your license key, when you enter it and about once a week) and checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com. Neither sends anything about your drives or files.

Updates and license checks

Ejectwise, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, which sees only the app’s version and, like any website, your internet address, and it downloads an update only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates). When you enter a license key, Ejectwise sends that key to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again about once a week; that is all it sends to Gumroad. Neither sends anything about you, your files or how you use the app.

Purchases

Ejectwise is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. The app sends your license key to Gumroad to check it (when you enter it and about once a week); that is all it sends to Gumroad.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
