The short version: Tumblemoon works fully offline and collects nothing unless you choose to play online.

Offline play sends nothing about you or your matches; the license and update checks are described below. Online play (optional, with the full game) needs no email, real name or password: our server keeps a made-up player name, your Mac’s public key, your online levels and results, and a scrambled form of your license key.

This policy explains how Tumblemoon (“the app”), a Mac game: an original platform fighter for 1 to 4 players, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

Nothing when you play offline (the license and update checks are described below). If you play online: a random account id (your online user ID), your player name and your Mac’s public key (to sign you in); your online levels, ranked results, leaderboard standing and online stats (matches and wins against players and against the game’s CPUs, tumbles, falls, damage, spikes, parries, your longest combo, streaks, the fighters you play and your last 20 ranked matches); a scrambled (keyed-hash) form of your license key with Gumroad’s last answer about it (valid or refunded; nothing else from Gumroad’s reply is kept); for each season, every fighter’s games and wins in player matches with no player attached (for the fighter tier lists); and, only for a match whose result is disputed, that match’s inputs for 30 days. Your network address is used while you’re connected, to carry the match, limit abuse and pick a server region near you, and isn’t saved. Tumblemoon has no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • Your story progress, medals, best times and unlocked colors, in a file in the game’s own folder on your Mac
  • Your last 20 replays (and the ones you star), and the clips you save
  • Your settings: controls, profiles, sound, graphics and accessibility options
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain
  • If you play online: the private half of your online player key, made on your Mac and kept in its Keychain (it never leaves this Mac; our server gets only the public half), and your account id and player name in the game’s preferences. The sign-in session is kept only while the game is open

Progress, replays and settings are in the game’s Application Support folder (named Tumblecrest, the game’s first name) and preferences, inside its sandbox; clips are saved where you choose; the license key and the online player key are in your Keychain.

Online play

Tumblemoon works fully offline. Online play comes with the full game. When you first go online, your Mac makes a private key that stays in its Keychain, and our server gives you a made-up player name (you can draw another). We don’t ask for an email address, a real name, your age or a password. Each time the game signs in online it sends your license key to our server, which checks it with Gumroad, our store; we keep only a scrambled form of the key, never the key itself. On our server we keep your player name, your Mac’s public key (how the server recognises you), your online levels and recent ranked match results, and that scrambled license key (to check your purchase and to keep one ranked player per license). Other players see your player name and level. Your online stats are public: anyone can look them up by your player name, in the game and on a web page (play.fitztoppicks.com/p/ followed by the name): your rank, matches and wins against players and against the game’s CPUs (kept apart), tumbles, falls, damage, spikes, parries, your longest combo, streaks, the fighters you play and your last 20 ranked matches with the other players’ names. Players in the top 50 of a ranked list are shown there with their name, rank, points and wins. Fighter tier lists (play.fitztoppicks.com/tiers) are worked out from everyone’s player matches and show no names. When nobody else is searching, ranked can match you with the game’s own CPU, always marked as a CPU. Matches pass through our relay, hosted on Cloudflare, so other players never see your network address; we use it only while you’re connected, to carry the match, to limit abuse and to pick a server region near you (from the rough area the address belongs to), and don’t save it. There is no text or voice chat. We don’t sell or share this data or use it for advertising. Sessions last a day; a lobby is gone minutes after the last player leaves; a disputed match’s inputs are kept for 30 days; everything else stays until you delete the player. Online › Account › Delete this online player removes everything from our server at any time, your ranked level too: nothing of it stays with your license or purchase, and a new player starts with placement matches.

Connection reports and reconnecting

Connection reports are sent only if you say yes. The first time you go online the game asks whether it may send them; you can change your answer any time in Online › Account › Connection reports. If you say yes, after each online match the game sends a few numbers about how the connection ran: the ping, the input delay, how often and how far the game had to correct itself, waits, reconnects, whether the match fell out of sync, and the game’s version. No player name, account, key, lobby code or network address goes with them. Our server adds them to daily totals per kind of match, keeps the totals for 90 days and uses them only to keep online play smooth. If you say no, nothing is sent. If you lose your connection in a match, our server keeps your place for 20 seconds and holds the match’s inputs while it runs (ranked matches in its storage until the match ends) so your game can catch up. Bought on the App Store? Your game sends Apple’s signed record of the purchase when it goes online; our server checks Apple’s signature and keeps only a scrambled form of the purchase’s id, to recognise refunds and keep one ranked player per purchase.

Network

Tumblemoon is sold directly through Gumroad. Besides online play (only when you choose it, with our server at play.fitztoppicks.com), its network requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the game opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the game’s product ID) and checks for game updates at fitztoppicks.com (a new version downloads from GitHub). No analytics, no ads.

Updates and license checks

Tumblemoon, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from GitHub, where this app’s downloads are kept, only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Tumblemoon contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Tumblemoon is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Tumblemoon is made for a general audience and isn’t marketed to children. Online play asks for no email, real name, age or contact details, has no chat, and player names are made up by the game. The identifiers it uses (the account id, your Mac’s public key, your network address and the sign-in session) are used only to run online play: to sign you in, carry matches, keep ranks, stats and leaderboards, and protect the service from cheating and abuse. They are never used for advertising, to contact anyone or to build an advertising profile, and are shared with no one except Cloudflare, which hosts our server. A parent can delete a player in the game (Online › Account › Delete this online player) or by emailing help@fitztoppicks.com with the player name.
