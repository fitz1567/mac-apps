The short version: Lessonbinder does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and none of your work is sent to us. The license and update checks are described below.

This policy explains how Lessonbinder (“the app”), a Mac app for private music teachers: students and families, a lesson calendar, invoices, practice sheets and recital programs, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Lessonbinder does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • Your studio file: students, families, lessons, makeup credits, practice logs, charges and payments you record
  • A backup each day
  • Your settings
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

The studio file is on your Mac, or in a folder you choose such as iCloud Drive. Settings are in Lessonbinder’s preferences and the license key is in your Keychain. Invoices, statements, sheets and programs are new files, written only where you choose.

Your files

Lessonbinder runs in Apple’s App Sandbox, so it can open and save only where you choose. It remembers the folder you picked for the studio file so it can keep saving there.

Calendar

If you turn on the Lessons calendar, Lessonbinder asks for Calendar access and writes your lessons to that one calendar in Apple Calendar.

Network

Lessonbinder is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the app opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the app’s product ID) and checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com. Neither sends anything about your students or families.

Updates and license checks

Lessonbinder, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from fitztoppicks.com only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Lessonbinder contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Lessonbinder is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
