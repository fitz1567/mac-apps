Reading Desk privacy policy

Effective October 6, 2026

Reading Desk is a Chrome extension by Kevin Fitzgerald. It does not collect your data. There is no account, no analytics and no server of ours: what you save stays in your browser on this computer.

What it keeps on your computer

  • Listen: your speed and voice choices, with Pro where you stopped in each article (page address, title and sentence number) and the articles you saved to listen later (title, address and text)
  • Easy view: your reading settings and presets, and with Pro the sites you chose for auto-apply
  • Speed read: your reader settings, and with Pro your reading history (page titles, site names, word counts and speeds) and where you stopped in long articles (page addresses)
  • Read later: the articles you save (title, link, readable text and images), your tags, folders, highlights and read/unread state, and reader settings
  • Print & PDF: your print settings, and with Pro the parts you removed on each site (the site's name and a short CSS selector such as aside.sidebar)
  • Recipe card: the recipe cards you open (kept in memory until Chrome closes), your unit setting, and with Pro your recipe box with tags, notes and each recipe's web address
  • Feeds: the feeds you subscribe to, your folders and settings, the items fetched from them (title, link, text) with read and starred state, and with Pro your keyword rules
  • Your license key, if you buy Pro

This lives in the browser's own extension storage. Removing the extension deletes it.

Network

Reading Desk never sends what you read anywhere: no account, no server, no analytics. Speech uses the voices on your computer. Online voices (such as the “Google” and “Natural” voices) are marked “online” and used only if you pick one or turn on “Let Automatic use an online voice” in Listen's settings; then the browser sends the text being read to Google or Microsoft to speak it. When you save an article, its images are downloaded (without cookies) so you can read offline; recipe photos are shown from the recipe site's own address; feeds you subscribe to are fetched directly from each site, without cookies. The only other request is checking a Pro license key with Gumroad, when you activate it and every two weeks.

Pro purchases

Pro is sold by Gumroad, which handles the payment and emails your license key. Gumroad's own privacy policy covers that purchase. When you activate a key, Reading Desk sends the key and Reading Desk's Gumroad product ID to Gumroad's license server (api.gumroad.com) to check it, and checks it again about every two weeks. Nothing else is sent.

Permissions

  • activeTab: Gives Reading Desk access to the current tab only when the user clicks its toolbar button or presses one of its shortcuts, so the page tools can work on that one page: Listen reads it aloud, Easy view restyles it, Speed read shows its text, Print & PDF cleans it up for printing, Recipe card reads its recipe data, Read later saves it, and Feeds lists the feeds it advertises.
  • scripting: After the user starts a tool, adds that tool to the tab: Listen's player and sentence highlight, Easy view's styles, tint and line ruler, Speed read's reader overlay, Print & PDF's toolbar and print styles (everything undone on exit), or a one-time read-only function that copies the article's text and images (Read later), the page's recipe data (Recipe card) or its feed links (Feeds). With Pro, Easy view re-applies itself on the sites the user chose. Nothing runs on a page before the user asks.
  • storage: Keeps each tool's settings and the user's own reading on this computer: speech speed and voices, places in articles and the listening queue; easy-view settings, presets and sites; speed-reading settings, history and places; saved articles with their text, images, tags and highlights; print settings and site rules; recipe cards and the recipe box; feed subscriptions, items and rules; and the Pro license.
  • sidePanel: Lets the user keep Reading Desk open in Chrome's side panel next to the article, for example to control easy view or listening while they read.
  • alarms: Checks the subscribed feeds for new items on a schedule (every 30 minutes, or 5–15 minutes with Pro). Nothing else runs on a timer.
  • unlimitedStorage: Saved articles keep their images so they can be read offline, and fetched feed items keep their text; a few dozen illustrated articles go past Chrome's default 10 MB local storage limit.
  • tts: Listen speaks the text with the speech voices installed on the user's computer (Chrome's text-to-speech API). Web pages can't use these voices from an extension's content script, so the service worker speaks for the page. Chrome shows no install warning for it, and it can't be an optional permission. (Firefox: the Web Speech API instead, no permission.)
  • http://*/*, https://*/* (optional): Never granted at install. Asked for one site at a time: when the user subscribes to a feed (that feed's site, to fetch it) and when a Pro user turns on Easy view's “Always use on this site” (that site, so easy view can apply itself there). Unsubscribing or turning it off gives the site back (unless the other tool still uses it); every granted site is listed and removable on the settings page.
  • https://api.gumroad.com/* (optional): Asked for only when the user activates a Pro license key, to check the key.
  • api.gumroad.com (asked only when you activate a key): to check your Pro license key.

Sharing and selling

Nothing is sold or shared. No data is used for advertising, credit or lending.

Children

Reading Desk isn't directed at children under 13 and collects nothing from anyone.
