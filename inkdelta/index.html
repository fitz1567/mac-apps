Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

Privacy PolicySupportAll apps
The short version: Inkdelta does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and none of your work is sent to us. The license and update checks are described below.

This policy explains how Inkdelta (“the app”), a Mac app that compares two versions of a Word (.docx) or PDF document and shows a redline, and compares spreadsheets (Excel, CSV and Numbers) cell by cell, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Inkdelta does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • Your compare settings (what to ignore, OCR, moved paragraphs)
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

Settings are in Inkdelta’s preferences and the license key in your Keychain. The documents and spreadsheets you open are read into memory while you compare them; reports, redlines and change lists you export are new files, written only where you choose.

Your files

Inkdelta runs in Apple’s App Sandbox, so it can open only the documents you pick or drop on it, and save only where you choose. It never changes the documents you compare. Scanned PDF pages are read with Apple’s on-device text recognition (OCR); nothing is sent anywhere. Printing goes through your Mac’s own print system.

Automation (Numbers files only)

Numbers files have no public file format, so when you compare a .numbers spreadsheet, Inkdelta asks the Numbers app on your Mac to export an Excel copy of that one file into Inkdelta’s own temporary folder, reads the copy and deletes it. macOS asks you once (“Inkdelta wants access to control Numbers”); Inkdelta can control Numbers and no other app, and only for files you chose. Nothing leaves your Mac. If you don’t allow it, export the file from Numbers yourself (File › Export To › Excel) and compare the .xlsx. To turn the permission off later: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation › Inkdelta.

Network

Inkdelta is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the app opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the app’s product ID) and checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com. Neither sends anything about your documents.

Updates and license checks

Inkdelta, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from fitztoppicks.com only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Inkdelta contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Inkdelta is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
