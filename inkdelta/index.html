Privacy Policy

Effective September 29, 2026

Privacy PolicySupportAll apps
The short version: Inkdelta does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and nothing about you or your work is sent to us.

This policy explains how Inkdelta (“the app”), a Mac app that compares two versions of a Word (.docx) or PDF document and shows a redline, and compares spreadsheets (Excel, CSV and Numbers) cell by cell, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Inkdelta does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac only:

  • Your compare settings (what to ignore, OCR, moved paragraphs)
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

Settings are in Inkdelta’s preferences and the license key in your Keychain. The documents and spreadsheets you open are read into memory while you compare them; reports, redlines and change lists you export are new files, written only where you choose.

Your files

Inkdelta runs in Apple’s App Sandbox, so it can open only the documents you pick or drop on it, and save only where you choose. It never changes the documents you compare. Scanned PDF pages are read with Apple’s on-device text recognition (OCR); nothing is sent anywhere. Printing goes through your Mac’s own print system.

Automation (Numbers files only)

Numbers files have no public file format, so when you compare a .numbers spreadsheet, Inkdelta asks the Numbers app on your Mac to export an Excel copy of that one file into Inkdelta’s own temporary folder, reads the copy and deletes it. macOS asks you once (“Inkdelta wants access to control Numbers”); Inkdelta can control Numbers and no other app, and only for files you chose. Nothing leaves your Mac. If you don’t allow it, export the file from Numbers yourself (File › Export To › Excel) and compare the .xlsx. To turn the permission off later: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation › Inkdelta.

Network

Inkdelta is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check with Gumroad (your license key, when you enter it and about once a week) and checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com. Neither sends anything about your documents.

Updates and license checks

Inkdelta, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, which sees only the app’s version and, like any website, your internet address, and it downloads an update only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates). When you enter a license key, Inkdelta sends that key to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again about once a week; that is all it sends to Gumroad. Neither sends anything about you, your files or how you use the app.

Purchases

Inkdelta is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. The app sends your license key to Gumroad to check it (when you enter it and about once a week); that is all it sends to Gumroad.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
