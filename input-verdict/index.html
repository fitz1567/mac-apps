Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

Privacy PolicySupportAll apps
The short version: Input Verdict does not collect your data.

No accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and none of your work is sent to us. The license and update checks are described below.

This policy explains how Input Verdict (“the app”), a Mac app that tests game controllers and other input devices and keeps a record of each unit you test, developed by Kevin Fitzgerald, handles information.

Information we collect

None. Input Verdict does not collect, store, sell or share personal information on any server, and it does not include analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools of any kind.

Information stored on your Mac

To work, the app keeps the following on your Mac:

  • The test sessions you save: the unit ID or serial, stage, ticket, notes, technician name (Shop) and the test results
  • Your settings: thresholds, display choices, keyboard layout and report branding (company name, contact line and logo, with Shop)
  • Your license key, in your Mac’s Keychain

Sessions are files in your user Library (Application Support, under com.fitz1567.InputVerdict), your settings are in its preferences and the license key is in your Keychain. None of it is sent to us. Settings › Privacy erases the saved sessions. PDF reports, exports and listing text are written or copied only when you ask.

Devices and permissions

Input Verdict reads game controllers through macOS’s GameController framework and joysticks, game pads and similar devices through IOKit HID. It never opens keyboards or mice as devices, so it needs no Input Monitoring or other permission. The keyboard and mouse testers only see key presses and clicks sent to Input Verdict’s own window while the tester is open.

Network

Input Verdict is sold directly through Gumroad, and its only network requests are the license check (when you enter a key, then each time the app opens and about once a week: Gumroad gets your key, and our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, gets a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself, to see whether it was reset; both get the app’s product ID), checks for app updates at fitztoppicks.com and the list of our other Mac apps, downloaded from fitztoppicks.com when you open it (at most once a day; the same file for everyone). None of them sends anything about your devices or test results.

Updates and license checks

Input Verdict, downloaded directly from Gumroad or fitztoppicks.com and signed with Apple’s Developer ID, checks for app updates with Sparkle, a standard open-source updater: about once a day it downloads a small update list (an appcast) from fitztoppicks.com, and it downloads an update from fitztoppicks.com only when one is available and you choose to install it (or have turned on automatic updates).

Until you enter a license key, Input Verdict contacts neither Gumroad nor our license page. When you enter one, it sends that key and the app’s product ID to Gumroad to verify it, and checks it again each time it opens and about once a week while it stays open. Gumroad’s answer (including the email address you bought with) is kept with the key in your Mac’s Keychain. With each of those later checks it also asks our license page, licence.fitztoppicks.com, whether the key was reset there (the page is where you free a Mac). That question contains only the app’s product ID and a fingerprint of the key (a one-way SHA-256 code made from it), never the key itself, your name or your email address, and the page doesn’t save it.

None of these requests contains anything about your files or how you use the app. As with any web request, the server also sees your internet address and ordinary details such as the app’s name and version, your macOS version and your language.

Purchases

Input Verdict is sold directly through Gumroad, which handles payment and sends your receipt and license key; we never see your payment details. What the license check sends is under Updates and license checks above.

Children’s privacy

Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that happens.
